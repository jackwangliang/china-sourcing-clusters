# Production cluster map

Last verified: 2026-11-10 · Grep tip: search by product word, Chinese place name, province name, or capability layer.

## How to use this file

Match the user's product or process to an entry, then produce a **Sourcing Route Card** as defined in `SKILL.md`. Always name the province, city and county/town, and state why the cluster formed there.

**Every entry below is indicative, not certified.** Cluster specialisation is stable over years, but boundaries shift, factories relocate, and a county known for one product may also host trading offices and warehouses. Use this map to decide *where to look and what to type*, never as proof that a specific company is a manufacturer. Confirm with public registration records and direct verification.

**No percentage or scale claims are made.** Marketing phrases such as "X% of world output" or "the capital of X" are deliberately excluded; they are common in promotional material and rarely verifiable.

### Sources used for this map

- 工业和信息化部《中小企业特色产业集群名单》(MIIT, national county-level cluster lists, including the 2023 / 2024 annual lists and the 2025 fourth batch / 2026 fifth batch announcements). Entries marked **MIIT** below are drawn from those published lists. Official site: `miit.gov.cn`.
- Provincial industry and information technology departments (for example Fujian, Shandong) publishing their own cluster lists and industry descriptions.
- Public county-level cluster directories and industry guides, including commercial cluster maps such as `tianxiagongchang.com/cluster`, used only as leads.
- Local government industry pages and trade-association descriptions.
- For smart-audio entries: MIIT list announcements as reported by 中国经济网 / 广州日报 (Shenzhen Longgang high-end intelligent vehicle devices, Futian smart terminals, Guangming smart transmission and sensing devices) and the 惠城区人民政府 notice on the 2025 fourth-batch list (Huicheng smart home appliances); plus local government industry reporting (深圳政府在线, 南方+) and published Pearl River Delta audio supply-chain investigations.
- The last group is used **only to establish the structure of a chain** — solution houses, electroacoustic components and whole-unit ODM — and the districts where those layers concentrate. No company mentioned in such reporting is endorsed, named or treated as verified here.

These sources establish that a county or district is associated with a category or a capability layer. They do **not** endorse any company, and they do not prove that any individual supplier operates a factory. Verify independently. For smart or connected goods, see "Products with a layered capability stack" below: the finished-good cluster answers only where assembly happens, not who owns the firmware or voice capability.

## Cluster vs market vs showroom

This distinction is the single most common sourcing error. State it explicitly whenever a trade hub appears in an answer.

| Place | What it actually is | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production cluster / 产业带 / 生产基地 | A county or town where the goods are physically made, with upstream suppliers nearby | The correct target for a factory search |
| Wholesale market / 专业市场 | A trading venue aggregating goods from many producing regions | Good for seeing variety; a stall is not a factory |
| Showroom, export office, platform storefront | A sales and marketing presence | Usually the first-tier-city intermediary |

Notable trade hubs that must not be reported as "the factory location":

| Hub | Province / city | Reality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 义乌 Yiwu International Trade City | Zhejiang · Jinhua | Small-commodity distribution hub. Some local manufacturing, but most goods arrive from other clusters. Excellent for discovery, weak for source-factory identification |
| 华强北 Huaqiangbei | Guangdong · Shenzhen | Electronics component trading district. Manufacturing sits in surrounding industrial districts and neighbouring Dongguan |
| 乐从 Lecong furniture market | Guangdong · Foshan | Furniture sales market; production is in Shunde and neighbouring towns |
| 中国轻纺城 China Light Textile City | Zhejiang · Shaoxing · Keqiao | **Dual role**: a fabric trading market *and* a genuine weaving / printing / dyeing cluster. Say which one is meant |
| Canton Fair and large general trade fairs | Guangdong · Guangzhou | Event, not a location of production. Exhibitor list mixes producers and intermediaries |
| B2B platform storefronts | n/a | Marketing presence; tells nothing about where goods are made |

## Products with a layered capability stack

A finished-good name is not always the right routing key. Smart speakers, AI toys, connected appliances, wearables, devices with an app, and anything with firmware, voice, AI, SDK or ecosystem requirements are assembled by one party while the capability the buyer actually wants is owned by another.

| Layer | Who owns it | How to route it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional solution (firmware, voice, AI, SDK, app) | Solution house, module maker, 机芯 / board supplier | Route by capability, often to an R&D district in a first-tier city or to a voice-technology hub outside the product's cluster |
| Backend platform, cloud service and data hosting | Software vendor, system integrator or service operator | **No cluster answer** — select by delivery model, data location, IP ownership and service terms |
| Chipset and module choice | Usually specified by the solution house | Do not route by cluster; ask who selects it |
| Key components | Component specialists | Their own cluster, which may differ from assembly |
| Whole-unit ODM (tooling, moulding, SMT, assembly, certification) | Assembly factory | The county-level production cluster |

Consequences for a Sourcing Route Card:

- Identify which layer carries the buyer's differentiating requirement and route that layer **first**; give the other layers as separate targets with their own Chinese queries.
- A supplier answering "yes, we can customise" may be subcontracting that layer. Ask which layer it performs in-house and name the producing entity.
- Prefer a **two-sided reverse lookup**: ask the solution house which factory runs its volume production, and ask the assembly factory who supplies its firmware. When both answers meet, the real chain is visible.
- Chipsets, cloud services and ecosystem certifications are chosen or held by a party in the chain, not found by searching a production county. List them as questions to ask, never as a location.
- **Some layers have no cluster answer.** A backend platform, admin console, cloud service, transcription dashboard, data-hosting arrangement, private deployment or ongoing subscription is not made in a county-level cluster. Say so explicitly instead of inventing a county. Select those layers by delivery model, data location, IP ownership and service terms; keep the geography route for the hardware layers.
- **Reverse the order when software decides the purchase.** Fix the backend / deployment specification first, choose the software or system supplier on capability, then source the hardware through the cluster route or reverse-lookup its contract manufacturer. The map answers where hardware is made, not who runs the service.
- Common error: reporting a county for a software, platform or data layer merely because the Route Card template asks for a primary cluster.
- **A first-tier-city address is not automatic proof of an intermediary here.** Genuine R&D solution houses and ODM headquarters sit in first-tier cities while their lines sit in surrounding industrial districts or neighbouring cities. Judge from the registered business scope and the production-site address.
- Common error: routing a smart product by its finished-good name and therefore reaching an assembly factory that does not own the firmware, voice or AI capability the buyer asked for.

See the smart-speaker and voice-solution rows in section C for an indicative example. Every layer still needs its own verification.

## Fallback when no entry matches

Never invent a cluster. Follow this sequence and label the conclusion **inferred**:

1. Decompose the product into its main material and manufacturing process (for example: stainless steel + forging + machining; or ABS + injection moulding + pad printing).
2. Build queries using `chinese-search-terms.md`: `<material/process> + 产业带`, `<product> + 生产基地`, `<product> + 生产厂家`.
3. Check the MIIT 中小企业特色产业集群名单 (national county-level lists) and the equivalent provincial lists for a matching county and category.
4. Check local industry-association pages, county government industry descriptions and public cluster directories for that specific product.
5. Locate the upstream input cluster (raw material, mould, casting, surface treatment, component). Final assembly is usually within a short distance of its suppliers — this is the defining feature of a 产业带.
6. Require at least three independent public mentions of the same county for the same category before naming it. Otherwise report the search route and say the cluster could not be established.
7. Even when a cluster is identified, still give the ranked channels, Chinese queries and verification steps. A location alone is not a sourcing result.

## Common reasoning errors

- Naming a province instead of a county. "Guangdong" or "Zhejiang" is not actionable; the town is.
- Assuming the largest trading market is the production base (see the table above).
- Treating a platform badge, an association membership or a promotional "capital of X" title as evidence of manufacturing.
- Concluding that a category has no Chinese production because English search returns only trading companies.
- Ignoring that one product may have several geographically separate stages (fabric in one county, dyeing in another, sewing in a third). State the stage being sourced.

## Maintenance

Each entry should keep: category, cluster at county/town level, a short structural note, and Chinese seed terms. When updating, prefer official cluster lists and local government industry pages, add or correct the `Last verified` date, and avoid adding market-share or output-volume claims.
