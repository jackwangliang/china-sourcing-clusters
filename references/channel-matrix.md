# Channel matrix

Last verified: 2026-11-09. Channel mechanics are relatively stable; platform badges, ranking rules, fees, event dates and access policies change. Recheck anything time-sensitive before acting.

## The selection rule

Rank channels by one question only: **does this channel let the buyer filter by where the supplier is registered and what it is licensed to produce?** Visibility is not quality. A polished English listing is evidence of marketing budget, not of manufacturing capability.

Export trading companies concentrate in first-tier cities and dominate channels that reward English content, paid placement and fast English replies. Cluster factories concentrate in county-level towns and are visible mainly through domestic-language channels, local markets, local industry associations and public registration records.

## Channel layers

### Layer A · Cluster-direct (highest factory density)

| Channel | What it is | How to reach the factory | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Domestic B2B marketplaces with a location filter | 1688.com and similar domestic wholesale platforms | Search in Chinese with the cluster place name plus factory-signalling terms; filter by seller location and business type; check the shop's registered entity | Chinese interface; many sellers still trade rather than manufacture; platform badges are self-declared |
| Local wholesale markets and industrial parks | Physical markets and parks inside the cluster (for example a lighting market in a lighting town, a hardware city in a hardware town) | Visit in person, or use the market's public tenant directory; ask each tenant which items it produces in-house | Requires travel or a local representative; a market stall is a sales point, not proof of a factory |
| Local industry associations and cluster administration | Trade associations, chambers of commerce and cluster management committees at county level | Use publicly published member directories and industry reports; ask about the local supply chain structure rather than for a named supplier | Often Chinese-language only; directories may be dated; rarely handles individual buyer enquiries |
| Chinese-language web search with a place name | General search using Chinese terms | Combine product + county/town + 生产厂家 / 源头工厂 / 加工 | Factory sites are often thin, outdated or absent; a weak website does not mean a weak factory |
| Local or vertical trade events | Region-specific and industry-specific exhibitions near the cluster | Look for exhibitor lists grouped by producing region; prioritise local pavilions over export-marketing booths | Dates and exhibitor lists change; verify from the organiser |

### Layer B · Export B2B platforms (mixed, filterable)

Alibaba.com, Made-in-China.com, Global Sources, HKTDC sourcing platforms and similar directories. These are efficient for discovering that a category exists and for collecting a first price band, but top-ranked results are usually the firms that pay for visibility, which correlates with trading companies and larger export-oriented factories rather than with cluster workshops.

To push toward the factory:

1. Filter by supplier location and compare it with the cluster identified in `cluster-map.md`. A supplier registered in Shanghai for a product made in a Zhejiang county town is an intermediary or a remote sales office until proven otherwise.
2. Read the business type and registered business scope, not the marketing headline.
3. Prefer listings that show production equipment, process detail and consistent specialisation over listings with a very broad catalogue.
4. Treat all platform verification badges as platform claims. Recheck the underlying entity in public records.

Main domain entry points are sufficient; avoid deep links that break. Do not bypass logins, CAPTCHAs, paywalls or anti-bot controls. If a page is inaccessible, drop it as evidence and fall back to a public entry point.

### Layer C · English-language search and general trade shows (highest intermediary density)

General web search, English business directories, LinkedIn, and large general trade fairs. These are where the visibility asymmetry is strongest. Use them to understand the category, terminology and typical specifications — not to conclude who the manufacturer is.

The Canton Fair and similar large fairs include both genuine manufacturers and export intermediaries. Check the exhibitor's registered address and business scope before treating a booth as a factory.

### Layer D · Public records (verification, not discovery)

Use these to confirm an entity found elsewhere, not to generate a supplier list:

- National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (国家企业信用信息公示系统) and commercial company registries — legal name, unified social credit code, registered address, business scope, registered capital, establishment date, shareholders
- Public patent and trademark records — evidence of technical activity; note the assignee name matches or not
- Public procurement and tender records, customs-related public information, job postings — signals of actual production activity, capacity and equipment
- Public maps and street-level imagery — whether the registered address is a factory site, an office building or a residential address

A registered address in a cluster is supporting evidence, not proof of manufacturing. Conversely a first-tier-city address is strong evidence of an office, not of a factory in that city.

## Choosing by order type

| Buyer situation | Sensible default |
| --- | --- |
| Custom / OEM / ODM, private tooling, repeat production | Go cluster-direct; invest in finding the actual producer and verifying equipment |
| Industrial component with a specification | Match by process capability and cluster, then verify certifications and inspection capability independently |
| Small first order, many unrelated SKUs, need consolidation | A trading company or sourcing agent may be the rational choice; make the markup and the role explicit |
| Need English contracts, export documentation, QC consolidation, credit terms | Intermediaries add real service; do not frame them as a mistake |
| Buyer cannot communicate in Chinese and has no local support | Budget for interpretation or a local representative rather than assuming an English listing equals a factory |

## Common mistakes

- Treating the biggest wholesale market as the production base. A market aggregates goods from many clusters.
- Searching only in English and concluding that no manufacturer exists for a niche item.
- Reading "manufacturer" in a company's own description as a verified fact.
- Comparing quotations from a trading company and a factory as if they were like-for-like; the scope of service differs.
- Building a shortlist before fixing the product specification. Nothing is comparable until the spec is fixed.
