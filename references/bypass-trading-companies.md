# Bypassing the intermediary: diagnosis and routes

Last verified: 2026-11-09. Company-registration practice and platform labels change; verify current requirements locally.

## Framing

A trading company is not a scam. Export intermediaries in Shanghai, Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Beijing, Hangzhou and other first-tier cities exist because they solve real problems: English communication, export documentation, consolidation of many SKUs, quality control, payment terms and logistics. They are visible in English because they invest in overseas marketing; cluster factories are not, because they generally do not.

The problem is **unlabelled intermediation**: the buyer believes a quotation came from a factory when it came from an office, and therefore cannot judge the markup, the real production capability, who owns the tooling, or who is accountable for a defect. The skill's job is to make the role explicit, then give a route to the producer when direct contact is worth the effort.

## Why English search reliably returns an intermediary

1. Overseas discoverability requires an English site, SEO, platform advertising and English-speaking sales — costs a cluster workshop rarely carries.
2. Platform ranking and trade-show placement favour firms that buy visibility.
3. Export documentation and single-point accountability lead overseas buyers to prefer one intermediary over ten small factories.
4. Many cluster factories deliberately sell domestically or subcontract to an exporter, and have no overseas sales function at all.

Result: the more polished the English presence, the weaker the evidence that the party operates a production line.

## Diagnostic signals

Treat these as prompts to verify, not as verdicts. Several signals together are meaningful; any single one is not.

| Signal | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Legal name contains 贸易有限公司 / 进出口有限公司 / 商贸有限公司 / International / Trading / Import & Export | Business scope in the public registry: does it say 生产 / 制造 / 加工 or only 销售 / 贸易 / 货物进出口? |
| Registered address in a first-tier city office district | Is there any production site at all? Ask for the producing entity's legal name and address |
| Extremely broad catalogue across unrelated categories | Factories usually specialise by process; a catalogue spanning socks and power tools suggests aggregation |
| "Our factory" with no factory name given | Ask which legal entity owns the production line and request its business licence |
| Only showroom or catalogue photos | Request a live video walk of the production line, raw material area, QC station and warehouse on a specified date |
| Vague or inconsistent answers on capacity, lead time, equipment, mould ownership | Ask for an equipment list and who owns the tooling |
| Certificate issued to a different legal entity | Compare the certificate holder's name to the contract counterparty |
| Payment to a personal account or to an unrelated entity | Treat as a hard stop until the counterparty is verified |
| No Chinese-language presence at all | Many real factories have a Chinese site, a domestic shop, or local directory entries; absence is worth checking but not decisive |
| Refusal to name upstream suppliers (mould, raw material, surface treatment) | Cluster producers normally know their immediate neighbours; refusal may indicate reselling |

## Evidence that is harder to fake

- Business licence showing manufacturing or processing in its scope, with a registered address inside the identified cluster
- Production licences, environmental or discharge permits where the process requires them (holder name must match)
- Equipment list, workshop layout, utility capacity and headcount consistent with the claimed output
- Patents or utility models assigned to the same legal entity
- Consistent Chinese-language trade records, local directory listings and job postings for production roles
- A physical site at the registered address that matches the claimed process
- Willingness to accept a sample order, pre-shipment inspection and a contract naming the producing entity

None of these is conclusive. State that they are documents to request and verify, never facts about a specific company.

## Routes to the producer

1. **Search by place, not by platform rank.** Use the cluster county/town name with Chinese product and factory-signalling terms from `chinese-search-terms.md`.
2. **Filter domestic marketplaces by location and business type.** Compare the seller's registered entity with the cluster; read the business scope.
3. **Use the cluster's physical infrastructure.** Local wholesale markets, industrial parks, trade associations and cluster management committees publish public directories and tenant lists.
4. **Follow the supply chain one step up.** Ask any candidate which mould maker, raw material supplier or surface-treatment shop it uses; cluster supply chains are local and named. Also useful when starting from a known finished product.
5. **Favour local pavilions at vertical trade events.** Exhibitor groups organised by a producing county are more likely to contain producers than general export-marketing booths.
6. **Verify in public records, then visit or commission an audit.** A third-party factory audit, a live video walk or an in-person visit is the step that converts a claim into evidence. Arrange this independently; this skill names no provider.
7. **Ask directly, in writing.** Put the question in the enquiry: "Are you the manufacturer? What is the legal name and address of the producing entity? Which processes are in-house and which are subcontracted?"

## When bypassing is the wrong advice

Advise against a direct-to-factory route, or at least state the cost, when:

- The order is small, one-off or combines many unrelated categories
- The buyer cannot handle Chinese communication, inspection, export documentation and consolidation
- The buyer needs credit terms, a single accountable counterparty, or certified documentation the factory cannot issue
- The product is regulated and the intermediary provides compliance handling

In those cases the honest answer is: the intermediary is worth the margin. Make the role and the margin explicit, and keep the factory-identification steps for later when volume justifies them.

## Short bilingual question set for an enquiry

Use these to establish who actually produces the goods. Keep the Chinese version verbatim; the English gloss is for the buyer.

| Chinese | English gloss |
| --- | --- |
| 请问贵公司是生产厂家还是贸易公司？ | Are you the manufacturer or a trading company? |
| 生产主体的公司全称和注册地址是什么？ | What is the full legal name and registered address of the producing entity? |
| 哪些工序是自有工厂完成，哪些是外协？ | Which processes are in-house and which are subcontracted? |
| 能否提供营业执照和经营范围？ | Can you provide the business licence and registered business scope? |
| 模具/工装归谁所有？ | Who owns the moulds and tooling? |
| 可否约定时间视频看车间、原料区和质检区？ | Can we schedule a live video walk of the workshop, raw material area and QC station? |
| 能否接受样品单和出货前验货？ | Do you accept sample orders and pre-shipment inspection? |

## Payment and transaction safety

- Verify the counterparty before any payment; the contract party, invoice party and bank account holder should match.
- Do not pay a personal account for a first order; do not accept "friends and family" style transfers.
- Use a sample order before a bulk order, and agree a written specification, inspection standard and defect remedy.
- Treat platform badges, uploaded certificates and self-declared "verified" labels as claims.
- Obtain independent legal and compliance advice where contracts, IP, tooling ownership, export controls, sanctions or regulated products are involved. This skill gives none.
